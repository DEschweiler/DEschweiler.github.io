Reassessing established reference values for morphometry on radiographs

Reference values that were established decades ago on few patients may not fully mirror what is seen in everyday clinical routine. A generalist framework for musculoskeletal measurements on radiographs puts them to the test on tens of thousands of images.

DetailsCloseDGMSR Science Award 2026
  • The framework I developed is generalist. Landmarks are placed once on a reference radiograph and transferred to every other radiograph automatically, so it is not tied to a single measurement or anatomy.
  • On more than 53,000 knee radiographs, we identified a concerning discrepancy between the traditional reference values and clinical routine for one measurement, the Insall-Salvati ratio.
  • The framework now also covers further anatomies and projections, as well as 3D data from MRI and CT. These extensions are implemented and currently being evaluated.

Segmentation and image generation for microscopy image data

A segmentation model can become an expert for your microscope without anyone annotating a single image. Image generation models make this possible by turning simulated cells into realistic, fully annotated microscopy images.

DetailsCloseWinner, Cell Segmentation Benchmark 2019
  • Segmentation means outlining every cell in a microscopy image. Doing this on a robust and general level is hard, because you need many annotated examples that cover different microscopes, imaging techniques and quality standards.
  • Simulating cell shapes is much easier, and a simulated shape is already its own annotation. A cell shape simulator can also be reused once it is set up, which makes the system sustainable.
  • What is still missing is the image that belongs to each shape. An image generation model adds it by turning the simulated cell into a synthesised microscopy image, which gives fully annotated image and mask pairs for training segmentation approaches.
  • To adapt this to a new microscope setup, you simply image your cells, train a diffusion model on them without supervision and let it turn simulated cells into cells that look exactly like the ones you just imaged.

A first step towards robots that assist medical staff

Medical staff are still exposed to radiation during some procedures. We built a system that lets a mobile robot with an arm map an unseen room, recognise what is in it and carry out tasks on request, as a first step towards handing such tasks over.

DetailsClose
  • The system combines mapping (SLAM), a labelled 3D model of the room and a vision-language agent that plans and carries out the next action, including simple object manipulation.
  • Target tasks are finding and unpacking sterile tools and materials, or monitoring radiation in an angiography suite.
  • Robots are becoming part of everyday life, and AI has reached a stage where they can advance quickly. As an engineer, I am particularly excited about this potential.

Natural-language control of clinical software

Clinical software can be operated simply by saying what you need. Our computer-use agents take over mouse and keyboard and work through the same interface a person uses.

DetailsClose
  • The agents only work with what is on the screen, so no programmatic access to the software is needed and the approach does not depend on the vendor or the age of the software.
  • Use cases are automating repetitive tasks, operating a system without touching it in sterile environments, and showing a user who is stuck how the system works.
